AWKWARD ELEVATOR

Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 6, 2026

Awkward Elevator ("the game") is made by Oktay Dak, an independent developer ("I", "me"). In short: the game collects no personal data. It has no accounts, no ads and no tracking. It sends a few anonymous gameplay counts (see section 3) that can't be tied to you. Your progress stays on your device, and Apple handles the one optional purchase.

1. Data stored on your device

The game saves your settings (music, sound effects, vibration, voices and reminders on/off), your progress (unlocked floors, stars, high scores, coins and shop upgrades) and whether you own the Full Game (a yes/no flag) only on your device. This data is never sent to me or to any server. Deleting the game deletes it. You can also erase your progress at any time in Settings → Reset Progress; this does not remove a purchase.

2. Reminders (notifications)

If you allow it, the game plans a few reminders on your device (for example a day, three days and a week after you last played) that mention where you left off, or a limited-time Full Game offer. They are scheduled locally by your phone: there is no push server, no notification token, and nothing is sent anywhere to deliver them. You can turn them off in the game's Settings → Reminders or in iOS Settings at any time.

3. In-app purchase

Floors 1-3 are free; the optional Full Game purchase unlocks the remaining floors. It is sometimes offered at a lower, limited-time price; either price unlocks the same content. Payment is processed entirely by Apple through the App Store. I never see or store your payment details, name, Apple Account or purchase history. The game only asks the App Store whether the Full Game is owned on your account (for example when you tap Restore Purchase) and remembers the answer on your device. See Apple's Privacy Policy.

4. Anonymous gameplay counts

To see how the game is played, it sends a handful of anonymous events to PostHog (PostHog Inc., data hosted in the EU): the game was opened (and whether it's the first open), a floor was started, cleared (with stars and score) or lost, the Full Game offer was shown, the Full Game was bought, a result card was shared (only that it happened, not where or with whom), the reminders question was answered, reminders or other settings were switched on or off, a reminder was tapped, and a limited-time offer started. The open event also says how far you got (highest floor reached), whether you own the Full Game, whether reminders are on, and how many whole days passed since the previous launch (counted on your device, so no id is needed). Result cards are drawn on your device and go only where you send them through the system share sheet. Each event carries only those game details and your platform (iOS). There is no account, device ID or advertising ID: every launch gets a new random session code that isn't saved, so separate launches can't be linked to each other or to you. The game asks PostHog not to look up your location, and client IP addresses are discarded. These counts are used only to improve the game, never for advertising, and are not sold or shared. See PostHog's Privacy Policy.

5. App updates

When the game starts, it may contact Expo's update service (EAS Update, operated by 650 Industries, Inc.) to download bug-fix updates. That request contains only the technical information needed to deliver the right update, such as the app version and platform, plus the network address every internet request has. I don't receive or store it, and it isn't used to identify or track you. See Expo's Privacy Policy.

6. What the game does not do

  • No accounts or sign-in; no name, email, contacts, location or photos are collected.
  • The only permission the game may ask for is notifications, for the optional reminders (section 2). No microphone, camera, location, photos or contacts.
  • No ads, no advertising identifiers, no crash-reporting services, and no analytics beyond the anonymous counts in section 4.
  • No tracking across other companies' apps or websites, and no data is sold or shared.

7. Children

The game does not collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13.

8. Your rights

Because I do not collect personal data, and the anonymous counts can't be linked to anyone, I hold nothing about you to access, correct or delete. Purchase data processed by Apple is covered by Apple's policies. For any question, contact me and I will help.

9. Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published on this page with a new effective date.
